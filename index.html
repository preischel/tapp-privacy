TAPP Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 September 2026

TAPP is a tap-based game with global leaderboards. This policy explains exactly what the app collects, why, and who else sees it.

Version 1.1.2 changes how TAPP serves ads and measures campaigns. The Unity, Meta and no-tracking details below apply to version 1.1.2 and later. Earlier versions use Google AdMob for ads and may ask for Apple's tracking permission; if permission is denied, their ads are not personalised.

The short version

You play first and save your account second. A TAPP account is a player name, a country, your scores and progress, and — once you save it — the sign-in you chose: Sign in with Apple or an email address. There is no password. We do not collect your real name, your phone number, your contacts or your location.

What we collect

You give us:

  • Username — the name you pick when you set up the app (or a generated one such as quickfox_21 on older accounts). It is shown publicly on the leaderboards, which is the whole point of it.
  • Country — a two-letter country code. Taken from your device's language and region settings when your account is created, and changeable by you at any time. Shown publicly next to your scores.
  • A sign-in, when you save your account — either your Apple ID, of which we receive only the email address Apple shares with us (Apple's Hide My Email relay address if you choose that), or an email address you type in. We use it for exactly one thing: emailing you a one-time code so you can sign back in. It is never shown to other players and is never used for marketing.

Created automatically when you play:

  • An account ID — a random identifier that lets our servers tell players apart. It is not derived from your device or from anything that identifies you personally. Saving your account attaches your sign-in to this same ID; it does not create a new one.
  • Gameplay events — for each run, the timings of your taps and, in Dodge, where on the screen you tapped. We use these to calculate and verify your score on our servers, which is how we keep fake scores off the leaderboards.
  • Your scores and results in Classic, Dodge and Reaction Time, and when you set them; your TAPP Points and streak.
  • Your subscription status — whether you have TAPP Pro, which plan, and when it renews or ends; and whether you bought the earlier one-time ad removal. We never see your payment details.

Only if you turn notifications on (Settings → Notifications, or "Remind me tomorrow" while setting up — both off until you choose them):

  • Notification preferences — which of the two switches you turned on, and your device's time zone, so a reminder arrives at a sensible local hour. Daily reminders are scheduled on your phone from your own progress; nothing about them is sent anywhere.
  • A push token — only if you turn on Competition alerts: a device token that lets us send you a notification when another player passes you. It is deleted the moment you turn the switch off, and with your account. We keep a record of each competition alert we sent (who was sent what, when) for 90 days so we never send the same one twice.

We do not collect: your real name, phone number, contacts, photos, or your location. TAPP never requests location access and has no location permission. Your country comes from a device setting and from your own choice — not from GPS, and not from your IP address. When you use Sign in with Apple we do not ask for your name.

Some of the companies below receive your device's IP address as a normal part of serving a request and may work out an approximate location (country or city) from it: TAPP's own website, to tell which country ads may be shown in (see "Where TAPP shows ads"; nothing is kept); Unity, for ads (never in the UK, EU/EEA or Switzerland); Meta, for app-install measurement; Google, for the website analytics you allow on tapptheworld.com; and our hosting providers, in their server logs. We have switched off IP-based location in our own analytics.

Who else receives data

  • Apple — if you use Sign in with Apple, Apple verifies your identity and passes us the email address you chose to share. Apple also handles all payments for TAPP Pro. See https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/
  • Supabase — hosts our database and our accounts, and sends the one-time sign-in codes to your email address. Your username, country, email address, scores and gameplay events are stored there, in the EU.
  • RevenueCat — manages the TAPP Pro subscription and the earlier ad-removal purchase, and tells our servers whether they are active. RevenueCat receives your account ID and your purchase history, never your card details. See https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy

  • Unity (Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads, version 1.1.2 onward) — our ad partner. It chooses and serves the banner and full-screen ads in free TAPP. Ads in TAPP are never personalised: they are not based on who you are or on what you do in other apps. Every ad request tells Unity to treat the player as a child for advertising purposes, that no consent to personalised advertising has been given, and not to sell or share their data. TAPP never gives Unity your name, email, account, username or scores.

    To show and count ads and to stop ad fraud, Unity stores and reads a little information on your device and receives device and network details: the device model, operating system, language, screen size, connection type, an app-specific device identifier (not Apple's advertising identifier), your IP address and the approximate country or city worked out from it, and which ads you saw and tapped. See https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

  • Google AdMob (versions before 1.1.2) — serves banner and interstitial ads in earlier releases. It may receive a device advertising identifier only if you granted Apple's tracking permission; declining does not stop ads, but they are not personalised. See https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • Meta — measures whether a Facebook or Instagram ad led to an install and records app launches so we can assess those campaigns. Meta receives basic device and app information. In version 1.1.2 and later, TAPP does not show Apple's tracking prompt or give Meta your advertising identifier, including if you allowed tracking in an earlier version. Meta's SDK also records App Store purchase, subscription and trial events for campaign measurement, but never receives your payment details. The app also tells Meta when this installation starts and finishes its first game, when a game is finished (and which game mode), and when the TAPP Pro screen is opened — never your name, email, account, username or scores. See https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/
  • PostHog — product analytics. In version 1.1.2 and later, it runs only if you turn Analytics on in Settings → Privacy (off until you do). Receives which screens you open and what you do in the app under a random device id that is not your account ID, so we can see what players use and what they get stuck on. It does not receive your email address or your name, and does not work out your location. Hosted in the EU. See https://posthog.com/privacy
  • Expo — delivers app updates and, if you turned on Competition alerts, relays those notifications: Expo receives your push token and the message text. See https://expo.dev/privacy
  • Apple also delivers any notification to your iPhone, as with every app.

If you have TAPP Pro (including a free trial) or bought ad removal, no ads are requested; if you already have it when TAPP opens, the ad software is not started at all. Meta's install and launch measurement remains active so we can tell whether an ad led to the installation.

Sharing a score builds the image on your phone and hands it to the app you choose through the system share sheet. Nothing about who you shared it with comes back to us.

Where TAPP shows ads

  • From version 1.1.2, in the UK, the EU/EEA and Switzerland TAPP shows no ads at all, and Unity's ad software is never started there.
  • From version 1.1.2, elsewhere (for example the US) free TAPP shows ads that are never personalised; TAPP Pro and the earlier ad removal have none.
  • How version 1.1.2 knows: when it starts, the app asks TAPP's own website (tapptheworld.com) which country the request comes from. The website works that out from your IP address, answers with just the two-letter country, and keeps nothing — no IP address, no identifier. The app keeps the country on your phone for up to a day. If TAPP cannot tell, it shows no ads. Your phone's region setting and your leaderboard country can also turn ads off (never on).
  • From version 1.1.2, TAPP never shows Apple's "Allow tracking" prompt and does not ask for your advertising identifier.
  • In version 1.1.2, Analytics is a separate choice (Settings → Privacy → Analytics) and has no effect on ads.

Keeping and deleting your data

Scores are kept indefinitely, because the all-time leaderboard is a permanent record.

Signing out keeps your account. Sign back in with the same Apple ID or email address and your name, scores, progress and subscription are exactly where you left them. An account you have not saved with a sign-in cannot be recovered if you delete the app, and the app tells you so.

Turning a notification switch off cancels every reminder on your phone and deletes your push token from our servers. Signing out does the same on that device.

You can delete your account from Settings → Manage account. Deleting it removes your sign-in, your username, your ranks and your entire score history from every leaderboard for good. The app warns you before doing this. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription — cancel that in your Apple ID settings.

If you would rather write to us, email myboroughuk@gmail.com from the address on your account, or tell us your exact username and country, and we will delete it for you.

Under UK and EU data protection law you have the right to access, correct or erase your personal data, and to complain to the ICO (https://ico.org.uk).

Children

TAPP is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 13. The app shows advertising and is rated accordingly.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, we will update the date above and note it in the app's release notes.
